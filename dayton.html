"The Conch Has Spoken: A Lord of the Flies Review"

My Opinion:

Personally, while Lord of the Flies itself was slow and not that entertaining, the great symbolism and strong (but debatable) theme make up for all of it. Golding uses well designed characters along with lots of hidden symbolism to the point where you could spend all day analyzing it. My personal favorite is the conch shell, representing the order on the island and the world that they left behind.

My Favorite Parts:

One of my favorite individual parts of Lord of the Flies is when Simon realizes the truth about the beast. This is shown when Golding writes:

“What I mean is…maybe it’s only us.” (Golding 89).

Going back to the conch shell I talked about earlier, it truly is one of the best symbols. Once it shatters near the end of the book, the order on the island completely falls apart, as Jack’s group starts chasing Ralph through the forest, throwing spears at him and even setting the forest on fire. Even if you don’t fully agree with Golding’s view, the point he makes here is strong: all humans have the capacity for violence inside of them.

The Theme:

The main theme in Lord of the Flies is that without societal rules to help guide them, humans would quickly lean towards savagery and violence. Golding uses the fear of the “beastie” to display this idea, and how the evil inside people isn’t from some outside source, but from humanity itself. The “beastie” represents all the fear, selfishness, and hunger for power inside them all.

While Golding makes a strong argument for his point, I don’t completely agree with it. The part that I do agree with is that in some cases, having fear and being power hungry can bring out the worst in people. But the fact that this would happen to everyone? Golding even disproves himself here with the existence of Ralph, Piggy, and Simon. While everyone else on the island has moved towards Jack and savagery, these three not only still care about logic and order, but even fight for these ideals as everyone opposes them.

Deeper Thinking:

  • One thing that I noticed is the huge contrast between Ralph and Jack. While Ralph represents more of the world they left behind, trying to get everyone to be responsible and follow rules, Jack uses fear and popularity to manipulate the island into joining him instead. While Jack uses immediate gratification to lure them in (the Id!), Ralph asks them to do menial work like building shelters and keeping the fire going.
  • Another thing that stood out to me is the irony of the ending. While the boys are rescued by adults, the adults themselves are also at war. This highlights that Golding may have been suggesting that what happened on the island could very well also happen in the real world. Now, this leaves the world with a difficult question to ask ourselves: Who’s going to be the ones to save us if this happens?
FINAL RATING: ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆, An amazing book with many deep themes that make you question the world around you.
