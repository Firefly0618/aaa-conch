"The Conch Has Spoken: A Lord of the Flies Review"

The Verdict

Honestly, while the pacing in the middle is as slow as a snail, the way the author develops the theme and introduces the characters is amazing. While I personally disagree with Golding's view of humanity, I see where he is coming from and how his life experiences could have led him to this conclusion. The characters are all well-designed, with many hidden meanings and other symbols, such as the conch and Piggy's glasses.

Textual Evidence: The Descent

One of the strongest moments in the novel comes when Simon realizes that the boys’ fear of the Beast is actually a fear of themselves. Golding writes:

“Maybe there is a beast... maybe it’s only us.”

This quote supports the idea that Golding sees evil as already present in humans, not caused solely by society. It connects to the symbols in the novel too: the conch represents order and civilization, while Piggy’s glasses represent logic and intelligence. As the boys destroy these symbols, they also destroy the rules that kept them human. Even though I do not fully agree with Golding’s negative view of humanity, this evidence shows why his argument is powerful. The characters’ descent from organized schoolboys into violence makes his theme clear and memorable.

Theme: Civilization vs. Savagery

One of the main themes of Lord of the Flies is that civilization is fragile, and without rules, people can quickly fall into violence and chaos. Golding uses the boys on the island to show that evil does not come from some outside monster. Instead, the “Beast” represents the fear, selfishness, and hunger for power that already exists inside people.

This connects to the symbols in the novel. The conch represents order, leadership, and democracy. Piggy’s glasses represent intelligence, logic, and the ability to create fire for rescue. As these symbols are weakened or destroyed, the boys become less civilized. Their descent shows Golding’s belief that rules and society are what keep human darkness under control.

I do not completely agree with Golding’s view of humanity because I think people are also capable of kindness, cooperation, and sacrifice. However, I understand his point. When the boys are scared, hungry, and free from punishment, many of them choose Jack’s power and excitement over Ralph’s responsibility and rescue plan. This makes the theme powerful, even if I think Golding’s view is more pessimistic than my own.

Deep Analysis (Bloom's Taxonomy)

  • Analyze: Golding uses the conflict between Ralph and Jack to show two different types of leadership. Ralph represents democracy, order, and long-term survival, while Jack represents fear, violence, and control. Their rivalry shows how quickly a society can break apart when people choose power and excitement over responsibility.
  • Evaluate: I think Golding’s message is powerful, but also very pessimistic. The novel makes a strong argument that humans can become cruel without rules, but it does not show as much of the good side of humanity. Characters like Simon and Piggy prove that kindness, truth, and intelligence still exist, even when the group becomes violent.
  • Reflect: If I were on the island, I would like to think I would stay loyal to Ralph and focus on rescue. However, Golding makes the reader question how easy that would actually be. Fear, hunger, and peer pressure affect the boys’ choices, which makes the story feel more realistic and uncomfortable.
  • Connection: Lord of the Flies connects to many modern stories about survival and social breakdown, such as The Hunger Games. Both stories explore what happens when people are placed in extreme situations and forced to choose between morality, survival, and power.
FINAL RATING: ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆ (4/5 Spears)
