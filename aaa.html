Textual Evidence

TTo understand why this book is so compelling, you have to take a look at how quickly civilization destruct from the inside. Golding doesn't just show the bad kids turning evil, he also shows the good kid kids losing their good.

"Ralph was full of fright and apprehension and pride. 'I wounded him! I hit him!'" (Golding 113).

This is a moment that present a civilized British boy changes, and violent is exposed. Additionally, the destruction of Piggy's glasses represents the total loss of logic and scientific thought on the island.

Theme: The Beast Within

Golding’s central message is terrifyingly simple: Human nature, when stripped of the threat of punishment, defaults to a struggle for power. The "Beast" isn't a physical monster; it’s the inherent capacity for evil within every person.

I agree with Golding's assessment to an extent. While I’d like to believe we are inherently good, history shows that fear is a more powerful motivator than logic. When the boys choose Jack’s feast over Ralph’s signal fire, they are choosing immediate gratification over long-term survival.

Deep Analysis (Bloom's Taxonomy)

  • Analyze: The contrast between Ralph (Democratic Leadership) and Jack (Autocratic Leadership) serves as a microcosm for 20th-century political tensions.
  • Reflect: If I were on that island, would I have the courage of Simon to speak the truth, or would I succumb to the pressure of the violent majority?
  • Connection: This story is the dark ancestor of modern media like The Hunger Games. It proves our fascination with societal breakdown is timeless.
FINAL RATING: ⭐⭐⭐☆☆ (3/5 Stars)
